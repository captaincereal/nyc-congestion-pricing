# Methodology

Frozen definitions are in `docs/project_brief.md`. This document is the detailed
statistical specification. Changes to a frozen item must be justified here with
a dated note.

## Treatment

CRZ tolling began on **2025-01-05** (`TREATMENT_DATE`). Treated units are DOT
speed links physically inside the Congestion Relief Zone (Manhattan ≤ 60th St,
excluding the exempt highways). Control units are comparable links outside the
zone, selected on pre-treatment behaviour (Phase 5). Boundary links are held
out of the control group and modelled separately for spillover.

## Primary outcome

Hourly **median** link speed in mph, aggregated from the sub-hourly feed to
`link_id` × hour. Median (not mean) to limit the influence of sparse extreme
readings within an hour. Mean speed and observation count are carried as
secondary / diagnostic series.

## Design 1 — Descriptive (Phase 6)

- Treated vs control median speed over time; treated−control difference over
  time; pre-treatment trends.
- Hourly profiles; weekday vs weekend; peak vs off-peak.
- Observation counts and link coverage over time.
- 2025-01-05 marked on every time-series plot. No causal claims.

## Design 2 — Difference-in-differences (Phase 7)

Two-way fixed-effects specification:

```
speed_{i,t} = alpha_i + gamma_t + beta * (treated_i * post_t) + eps_{i,t}
```

- `alpha_i`: link fixed effects.
- `gamma_t`: time fixed effects (date-by-hour, or day + hour-of-week).
- `beta`: average treatment effect on the treated (ATT), in mph.
- Standard errors clustered by `link_id`; two-way (link and date) as a
  robustness check.
- Unit of observation: link × hour. Estimated on the full panel and,
  separately, on peak-only / off-peak / weekday / weekend subsets.

### Identifying assumptions

- **Parallel trends**: absent treatment, treated and control links would have
  evolved similarly. Assessed via pre-period event-study coefficients — not
  assumed proven.
- **No anticipation**: behaviour does not change before 2025-01-05. Tested with
  leads.
- **SUTVA / spillovers**: diversion to boundary links violates SUTVA; handled by
  (a) excluding boundary links from controls and (b) a separate spillover
  regression on boundary links.
- **Stable measurement**: the probe panel and link definitions do not change
  discontinuously at the treatment date. Checked in the data-quality report.

### What beta does and does not imply

`beta` is the average change in hourly median speed on treated links,
differenced against control links, over the post period. It is **not** a
statement about any single corridor, about volume, about travel time
door-to-door, or about welfare. Practical significance is judged separately
from statistical significance (mph change relative to baseline speed).

## Design 3 — Event study (Phase 8)

```
speed_{i,t} = alpha_i + gamma_t + sum_{k != -1} theta_k * 1[event_time_{i,t} = k] + eps_{i,t}
```

- `event_time` in weeks relative to treatment (months as a robustness check).
- Reference period `k = -1`.
- Plot `theta_k` with 95% CIs: flat, near-zero pre-period coefficients support
  parallel trends; the post-period path shows the dynamic adjustment.
- If pre-period coefficients are jointly significant or trending, the parallel-
  trends assumption is not credible and causal language is withheld.
- Sun & Abraham (2021) / Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021) only if treatment timing
  effectively varies (e.g. staged enforcement) — not expected here.

## Robustness (Phase 9 — separate specifications, not edits to the primary)

- Alternative control groups (outer-borough only; matched on pre-treatment
  speed; synthetic control).
- Controls farther from the toll boundary.
- Alternative pre/post windows; drop the ±2-week transition around 2025-01-05.
- Placebo treatment dates in the pre-period.
- Peak vs off-peak; weekday vs weekend.
- Exclude poor-coverage links and known outage periods.
- Alternative aggregation (daily; link-week).
- Weather controls in/out (added only if descriptive analysis shows imbalance).

Each produces a row in a comparison table showing whether the conclusion
materially changes.

## Reproducibility

- Fixed `RANDOM_SEED = 20250105`.
- DuckDB SQL builds staging and the panel deterministically; Python consumes
  `data/processed/`.
- Each table/figure regenerated by a single `python -m src...` call.
- See `docs/reproducibility.md`.

## Decision record

### 2026-09-08 — Primary data source changed to the EZ Pass local-street feeds

**Status:** approved by the project owner on 2026-09-08.
**Frozen item affected:** primary data source only. The research question,
primary outcome, treatment date, unit of analysis, and estimation strategy are
**unchanged**.

#### Why the original source cannot answer the question

The project began on NYC DOT "Traffic Speeds NBE" (Socrata `i4gi-tjb9`). A full
census of the 40 months ingested (2023-01 .. 2026-04, 40.2M rows) shows that
feed carries only **~121-125 distinct `link_id`s city-wide**, stable in every
month; the ~1M rows/month are the same ~123 segments re-read every ~5 minutes.

Parsing `link_points` and selecting links whose full extent lies below ~60th St
in Manhattan returns **18 links**, and every one is either exempt from the toll
or a crossing into the zone:

| Count | Links | Status under CRZ tolling |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | FDR Drive | **Exempt** — excluded roadway |
| 4 | West Side Hwy / West St / 11th & 12th Ave | **Exempt** — Route 9A excluded |
| 4 | Lincoln Tunnel tubes | Tolled *entry point*, not a zone street |
| 2 | Queens-Midtown Tunnel | Tolled *entry point* |
| 4 | Brooklyn Bridge / BQE approaches | Approach, mostly Brooklyn side |

There are **no** links on Broadway, 5th/Park/Madison/Lexington, 34th/42nd/57th,
Canal, or Houston. Under the frozen definition ("treated = links inside the
CRZ") the treated group is therefore empty, or worse, composed entirely of
roadways the toll does not apply to. Estimating the frozen specification on
these links would measure the effect of congestion pricing on roads exempt from
congestion pricing. This is a validity failure, not a precision problem, so no
amount of additional `i4gi-tjb9` data fixes it.

#### The replacement

Two Socrata datasets published by NYC DOT from E-Z Pass readers on **local
streets**, with identical schemas and the same 351 `sid`s (range 1004-204047),
which join with no gap:

| Dataset | Coverage | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| `erdf-2akx` — EZ Pass Readers 2021-July 2024 | 2021-04-08 .. 2024-07-07 | 108.4M |
| `6a2s-2t65` — EZ Pass Readers July 2024-current | 2024-07-08 .. 2026-09-07 | 82.1M |

Columns: `sid`, `link_name`, `borough`, `polyline` (encoded polyline geometry),
`link_length_ft`, `aggregation_period_sec`, `n_samples`,
`median_calculation_timestamp`, `median_tt_sec`, `median_speed_fps`.

Why it satisfies the frozen design where `i4gi-tjb9` does not:

- `median_speed_fps` **is** the frozen primary outcome (median link speed), not
  a proxy or a derived quantity. Converted to mph; hourly medians are taken over
  the 15-minute segment medians within each hour.
- Segments are genuine tolled CRZ surface streets — e.g. "57th Street -
  Eastbound - 6th Ave to 5th Ave", "23rd Street - eastbound - madison ave to
  3 ave", "Lexington Ave - Southbound".
- 351 segments across Manhattan, Queens, Brooklyn and Staten Island (no Bronx),
  giving a real treated group *and* a real pool of outside-zone controls.
- ~3.75 years pre-treatment and ~20 months post-treatment, exceeding the two
  pre-treatment years the design requires for parallel-trends testing.
- `polyline` supports geometric CRZ assignment; `n_samples` supports coverage
  filtering; `link_length_ft` and `median_tt_sec` allow an independent check of
  the reported speed.

#### Consequences for the specification

None to the estimator. `RAW_SEGMENT_COL` becomes `sid`, `RAW_SPEED_COL` becomes
`median_speed_fps` (mph-converted), and `RAW_TIME_COL` becomes
`median_calculation_timestamp`. Treatment assignment moves from `link_points` to
`polyline`, and must be geometric — **not** string matching on `link_name`.

#### Retained use of `i4gi-tjb9`

The 40+ months already ingested are retained and **demoted to a secondary
source**. FDR Drive and the West Side Highway are precisely the toll-exempt
roads onto which traffic could divert, and the tunnel/bridge links are the
tolled entry points. They therefore support the spillover/diversion analysis
(Phase 9 robustness and the SUTVA discussion above) at 5-minute resolution over
the full study window. This is a genuine addition to the design, not a salvage.

### 2026-09-09 — Rolling-window downsampling of the EZ Pass feed

**Status:** ingestion/sampling decision. **No frozen item is affected** — the
outcome remains the hourly median link speed.

The EZ Pass feeds do not publish independent 15-minute observations. They
publish a **rolling** 900-second median, re-emitted roughly every 61 seconds.
Measured on segment `sid=1004` for 2025-01-06 08:00-09:00: **52 readings in the
hour, median gap 61 s**, each summarising the preceding 900 s. Consecutive rows
therefore share ~14 of their 15 minutes (~93% overlap) and are frequently
byte-identical — eight consecutive readings returned `median_speed_fps = 20.57`.

Ingesting every row would mean ~10.4M rows *per month* (~450M over the study
window) of which ~92% carry no independent information, and would take days
against a feed that throttles sustained pulls.

Ingestion therefore restricts to the minutes that open each **non-overlapping**
15-minute window, plus a backup minute in case the first is missing:
`date_extract_mm(median_calculation_timestamp) IN (0,1,15,16,30,31,45,46)`.
Staging then keeps exactly one reading per `(link, 15-minute window)`,
preferring the median built from more probe samples.

Measured effect on one hour of live data: 12,938 rows -> 1,978 rows (**6.5x**)
with **all 287 active segments retained**. A stricter 4-per-hour filter gives
13.2x but drops to 274 segments, so the backup minute is kept.

This is a reduction in redundancy, not in information: the retained series is
the set of independent 15-minute medians from which the frozen hourly median is
supposed to be computed. Medianing 52 overlapping windows would in fact
over-weight whichever value persists longest, so the downsampled series is the
more defensible input. The filter is a one-line change in
`src/config.py:EZPASS_SAMPLE_MINUTES` should a future check want the full feed.

Implementation note: SoQL's minute function is `date_extract_mm`.
`date_extract_m` is **month** and silently matches nothing — it returned zero
rows without erroring during development.

#### Known data-quality issues to resolve in Phase 3

- `median_speed_fps` contains impossible values (observed max ~20,662 fps,
  ~14,000 mph). Outlier rules must be set and documented before any estimation.
- `aggregation_period_sec` takes two values (0 and 900); restrict to 900.
- ~190M rows across both datasets. Ingestion filters server-side to the study
  window (`>= 2023-01-01`) and to `aggregation_period_sec = 900`.
- Socrata throttles sustained bulk pulls; ingestion is month-partitioned,
  resumable, and manifest-checked per month.
